*Final, 2026-08-20. Not a rewrite. ## 0. The one-paragraph answer

The deep literature **confirms the playbook's P0 spine** — product correct on the ICP's names → first session → talk to every user → count, don't measure → one channel for 90 days → ≥10 organic payers → then pour; no paid until the gate; engineering behind ≥6 interviews; COGS/licence as a P0 blocker. Graham, Ellis & Brown, Lemkin, Traynor, Verna, Balfour, Walling and Cohen arrive there by different roads, and that convergence is itself a finding. **Honest novelty count: of the 18 items in §1, about seven are new to the repo** — the time-to-shortlist interview question (2), the "alert room you can't audit" copy target (3, half-new), the regulator-defined engagement no-list (7), "founder ≠ ICP" as the first interview risk (10), the Sales Safari method (11), the behavioural-finance positioning "confidence, control, attention triage — process, not edge" (15), and the s911A(2)(ea) "principal purpose" lawyer question with the Simply Wall St CAR comparison (§6). One more (1, the opt-in-public record as the only plausible network effect) is new but P2. The remaining ten already sit in the playbook, `GROWTH_ANGLES.md`, `COMPLIANCE_COPY_RULES.md` or `PAID_ADS_PATHWAY.md` and are listed here as re-framings with better citations, not discoveries. Two repo-state claims in the draft were wrong and are corrected: the personal track record is **live for Premium today** (so trialists see it; no flag exists), and a **first-party AI connector already shipped** (MCP server, #511/#516). **Change to the 30/60/90 plan: small** — wording in the interview script, lawyer brief, OPERATING_RULES and COMPLIANCE_COPY_RULES; a capped Safari step inside item 2; one guardrail metric; three LATER rows; one dated decision inside the existing 2027-05-31 re-decision. Net ~+10–14 founder-hours over 90 days, 0 engineering-days. The order of operations does not move.

**What this means for "solo founder, 20 users, 0 paying, month ~3–4":** unconditionally, more than half of comparable products never take a dollar and ~5 % reach ~$8–10k MRR; conditional on eventually succeeding, the median product is *also* under $1k MRR at month 3–4. So **0 payers now is uninformative on its own. **The OMTM question this raises — answered.** The diagnosis above is "visits"; the playbook's One Metric That Matters is "activated trialists returning in week 2". Position taken: **the OMTM does not change for the next 90 days** — an unvalidated product fed more visits wastes the only scarce thing (the handful of trialists who can be interviewed cleanly), and the 60-day content cadence is already the visits lever. **But a guardrail is added with a number:** weekly unique visitors, counted by hand alongside the OMTM. If, with the cadence held, weekly uniques have not roughly doubled (~50 → ~100/week) by the day-90 review, the month-4 channel review treats top-of-funnel as the OMTM for the following quarter. That converts the diagnosis into a dated decision without re-ordering the plan.

## 5. What Tapeline is really selling, per behavioural finance

**Base rates (B unless noted):** households that traded most earned 11.4 %/yr vs market 17.9 % (Barber & Odean, *JF* 2000); stocks retail investors buy underperform those they sell by ~3.3 pp over 12 months (Odean, *AER* 1999); disposition effect ~1.5× (Odean, *JF* 1998); going online — more data, more control — turned market-beaters (+2 %) into laggards (−3 %) via "illusions of knowledge and control" (Barber & Odean, *RFS* 2002); Robinhood top-bought stocks averaged **−4.7 %** 20-day abnormal return, −9 % after extreme herding events (Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, *JF* 2022); illusion of control is inversely related to professional traders' performance (Fenton-O'Creevy et al. 2003).

**Why people pay anyway (A):** subjects pay for information about whether their decision is ex-post optimal *even when it cannot change the decision* — the "confidence effect" (Eliaz & Schotter, *GEB* 2010); the average participant sacrifices 8–15 % of expected earnings to retain control of the payoff (Owens, Grossman & Fackler, *AEJ:Micro* 2014); more information raises confidence without raising accuracy (Oskamp 1965 → Barber & Odean 2002).

**Therefore:** a 500-filter scanner is an illusion-of-knowledge machine; a one-number scanner is a confidence machine. Neither sells returns. Willingness to pay comes from confidence + control + attention triage — which is exactly why "published track record" does not appear in positive reviews (gap analysis) and why the competitor set sells *volume of tools* (more control cues). The honest and durable version also *reduces* the documented harm: fewer decisions, a process (watchlist + alerts + a live but shallow Free tier), a score framed as a report card on the tape rather than a forecast (Morningstar's long-standing framing — paraphrased, wording [unverified]). **Copy consequence (descriptive):** "one number, one sentence, and a public record of what happened next — so you look at fewer things, decide once, and stop refreshing." Market the scanner as a pre-commitment device, not a trigger. This is also the behavioural-finance-correct posture: the one thing the data say unambiguously is "trade less, with a process." Interview script addition: ask for the emotional and social job ("what do you want to feel after the evening review? who do you tell about a name?").

## 6. The track-record bet, re-examined against Hulbert / Morningstar

**Do records predict? No.** Newsletter recommendations do not outperform; raw persistence vanishes in abnormal returns (Jaffe & Mahoney, *JFE* 1999, B); no stock-picking ability, no hot hands across 153 newsletters (Metrick, *JF* 1999, B); the Hulbert Financial Digest's 1981–2010 aggregate lagged the S&P 500's 12.1 % by ~3.7 pp/yr (B); Morningstar 5-star ≈ 3-star forward (Blake & Morey 2000, B); only 12 % of 5-star funds kept 5 stars over the next 5 years (WSJ "Morningstar Mirage" 2017-10-25, B); Value Line Timeliness faded from ~2000 (CXO, B).

**Do records sell? Not the audited kind — the salient kind does.** The Hulbert Financial Digest, the only independent auditor of newsletter records, ceased publication in January 2016 because the market stopped paying for it (D/C). But a discrete Morningstar star change itself drives flow: an initial five-star rating produced average six-month abnormal flow of $26M, **53 % above normal expected flow** (Del Guercio & Tkac, *JFQA* 2008, B — confirmed); advertised past performance attracts inflows with no subsequent outperformance (Jain & Wu, *JF* 2000, B); top-rated funds drew the large majority of dollars despite non-predictive ratings (WSJ 2017).

**How the durable score brands earned belief (C synthesis):** Morningstar (1985–), Value Line (1965–), Zacks (1988–) framed the score as backward-looking / a first-stage screen; conceded modest or no predictiveness when pushed; disclosed hypothetical returns in plain words (Zacks' rank-disclosure page says, in substance, that the published rank returns are not achievable with actual portfolios — wording [unverified], go.zackspro.com/rank-disclosure); earned trust via independence, longevity and intermediary distribution (advisers, libraries, syndication); never told the retail reader what to do. The one that marketed its record (Motley Fool) is the one whose record is the standing controversy.

**Reconciled with Tapeline's own finding** (no positive review cites a record; records appear in cancellations — `COMPETITOR_GAP_ANALYSIS.md`): a record is a **disconfirmation asset** — buyers do not shop on it, churners use it to justify leaving after the first drawdown (exactly what mean-reverting short-horizon records predict). It *does* work when simple, salient, perceived as third-party, and consumed by intermediaries — press, reviewers, AI assistants — which is the Morningstar mechanism and the AEO mechanism (§1 item 17).

**What changes:** keep the public scorecard; make it more honest (full distribution, misses, not-a-portfolio / not-net-of-costs caveats); move it to the "why trust us" position and into third-party-facing assets (press kit, /developers, /mcp, Reddit answers, AI-readable pages) and to where doubt lives (pricing page, trial-expiry email, cancel flow); **never lead with hit rate or median alpha as a benefit — lead with "we publish it, including the misses" as a character claim.** This now also governs the Meta copy bank (`META_ADS_DECISION.md` §9), which the draft had contradicting this section. It is the FTC/SEC/ASIC-safe framing: every 2022–24 US action (FTC v. Warrior Trading $3M, Raging Bull $2.425M, WealthPress $1.7M; SEC Marketing Rule sweeps 2023-173 / 2024-46 on hypothetical performance to the public) is an earnings-claim / hypothetical / touting case. The closest US precedent to Tapeline's shape is *Lingley v. Seeking Alpha*, No. 1:23-cv-05849 (S.D.N.Y., Marrero J., **2024-08-15**): a **pleading-stage dismissal with leave to amend** on the Advisers Act publisher exclusion — quant ratings + filters + alerts still "impersonal, disinterested, regular" publishing (gtlaw.com, katten.com summaries). **It is not settled: a Second Circuit summary order issued 2026-08-06 in *Lingley v. Seeking Alpha*, No. 24-2437 (Leval, Wesley, Sullivan), outcome not read this session; summary orders are non-precedential.** Do not describe the 2024 dismissal as "the publisher win" in anything the lawyer reads until that order is read — it is lawyer-brief item (i) below. The playbook's item-27 decision point (drop the record from value themes if share-beat-SPY <50 % at n≥250) stays; this addendum says the record should already be in the reassurance position regardless.

**Australia — the open risk, stated for the lawyer brief.** The exemption is s911A(2)(ea)–(ec) with a "sole or principal purpose is not the provision of financial product advice" limb; ASIC's 2025–26 posture is "licence or authorised rep" (18 finfluencer warnings June 2025; 26-081MR 2026 [unverified]); every AU score comparable — Simply Wall St (CAR of Sanlam, AFSL 337927), Motley Fool AU (AFSL 400691), Morningstar AU (AFSL 240892) — is licensed or an authorised rep and carries the s949A warning. Tapeline's exemption reliance is the outlier posture: not obviously wrong (US-market, impersonal, descriptive) but the precise questions to ask verbatim: (a) does an AU-incorporated publisher of US-stock scores to predominantly US users "carry on a financial services business in this jurisdiction"; (b) does a score-only product fail the "principal purpose" limb; (c) is a CAR arrangement like Simply Wall St's the cheap fix; (d) may summary statistics (hit rate, median alpha) appear in acquisition copy at all; (e) may a founding-member **presale** (taking money before delivery, from an AU entity relying on the exemption) be run as a validation test — Cohen's "20–30 customers waiting to pay" is the canon's bar, but it is a money-taking act and belongs on the brief before it appears as a test; (i) the 2026-08-06 Second Circuit order above.

## 7. The price / trial question — the one open disagreement

CLAUDE.md: "Three-tier price points … only revisit with conversion data." The bootstrapper canon (Walling, Cohen, McKenzie — C/D, survivorship-filtered) says the opposite: price is the first design variable; re-price on a 6–12-month clock; card-up-front or a 60-day guarantee over a no-card trial for a bootstrapper who "can't handle high churn"; the ~$9 B2C tier is the "cheap" middle that attracts the highest-support, lowest-loyalty segment (McKenzie: ~80 % of support requests, ~95 % of headaches); and at ~7 signups/month the conversion data that would trigger the CLAUDE.md rule will not arrive. The canon-consistent shape for this stage is **price dear**: one Premium-ish tier in the $39–59 range [competitor list prices unverified] for the narrower already-paying comparison shopper, card-up-front or 60-day money-back, annual at two-months-free — the Trader $59 SKU already exists as the anchor. The draft's second shape — "price free and monetise the audience" (StockAnalysis / Finviz model) — is **not an option at ~200 visits/month; it is a different company**, and is dropped (P2 at earliest, and only after a traffic order of magnitude the plan does not forecast).

This addendum does **not** recommend changing price now (rule: strategy changes ≤1 per quarter; the interview gate is unmet). Playbook G15 already fixes the date: **2027-05-31, <10 organic 60-day payers → change product/ICP, change price/anchor, or stop.** The add is to write the *shape* next to that date in `OPERATING_RULES.md`: "at that review, if <10 unaffiliated payers with 60-day retention, re-decide price/trial toward the dear shape above or shelve" — a pre-committed kill criterion (Walling), not a reaction. A founding-member presale as the conversion test waits on lawyer-brief item (e).
