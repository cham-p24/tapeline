# Customer interview script

*The 15-minute call that fills `FEEDBACK_LOG.md`. Written 2026-08-30.*

`OPERATING_RULES.md` §1 gates every 30/60/90 engineering item on **≥ 6 recorded interviews**, with a
milestone of **2026-09-20**. `FEEDBACK_LOG.md` already defines the fields. This file is the missing
half: what to actually say to get them.

---

## Before you start: the cheapest interviews are already sitting unread

`FEEDBACK_LOG.md` records that the **2026-08-11 email to 17 users asked five questions, and the
replies are in `christian@tapeline.io`, unread and unlogged.**

Read that inbox before booking a single call. A reply is a shorter interview that has already
happened, and logging one costs minutes rather than a scheduled 15. **If four people replied, you
are two calls from the gate, not six.**

Then: `OPERATING_RULES.md` §7 — every new signup gets a same-day human reply with one behaviour
question and a 15-minute call offered. That is the pipeline. This script is what happens when
someone says yes.

---

## The two rules that bind this call

**1. Never record — and do not ask — anything under compliance rule 8.**

No capital, account size, holdings, positions, experience level, risk tolerance, or goals. Not as
small talk, not as rapport, not "just to understand you better". If someone volunteers it, hear it
and move on; it does not go in the log.

This is not squeamishness. Those are the suitability questions that were stripped from the product
on 2026-07-18, and collecting them in a call recreates exactly the record the publisher-exemption
posture depends on not having. **A transcript is a record.**

**2. Descriptive voice, on a call as much as in the product.**

Never say *buy*, *sell*, *you should*, or *we recommend* — not about a ticker, not hypothetically,
not as a joke. If they ask "so would you buy XYZ?", the answer is the same one the product gives:
*"I can tell you what the score is and what's driving it. What you do with it is yours."*

Do not sell during the interview. The moment you start pitching, they start being polite, and
polite answers are worthless.

---

## Opening (60 seconds)

> "Thanks for making time. This is me trying to understand how you actually work, not a demo — I'm
> not going to pitch you anything. There are no right answers, and 'I stopped using it' is genuinely
> the most useful thing you could tell me. Mind if I take notes?"

If recording, ask. If they hesitate, take written notes instead — a hesitant yes costs you candour
for the rest of the call.

---

## The questions

Six blocks, ~2 minutes each. **Ask them in this order.** Alternatives before Tapeline, always —
once you have mentioned your own product, every later answer is anchored to it.

### 1. The alternative — the single most valuable field

> "Before Tapeline, what were you using to find names worth looking at?"
>
> Then: **"Walk me through the last time you used it."**

The second question is the one that works. "What do you use?" gets a tool list; "walk me through the
last time" gets the truth, including the spreadsheet they are slightly embarrassed about.

Push once for a name: Finviz free? Trade Ideas? TradingView screener? A spreadsheet? A Discord?
Nothing at all? **"Nothing" is a real and important answer** — write it down rather than helping
them find one.

*Log: `Alternative named`.*

### 2. The workflow moment

> "When in your week does that happen? Take me to the actual moment — where are you, what time is
> it, what just made you go looking?"

You are after the trigger, not the schedule. "Sunday night with coffee before the week" and
"whenever I see something moving on X" are different products.

*Log: `Workflow moment`, in their words.*

### 3. First contact with Tapeline

> "When you first landed on Tapeline — what were you expecting to see?"
>
> Then: **"And did you find it?"**

Then shut up. The pause is where the real answer lives.

If they did not find it, get specific: *"Where did you look?"* That is a navigation bug report
disguised as a feelings question.

*Log: `Hoped to see first`, `Found it?`.*

### 4. What made them stop

Only if they lapsed — which is most people, and it is fine to say so plainly:

> "You signed up and then didn't come back much. I'm not offended — what happened?"

Then be quiet. Do not offer them an excuse ("too busy?", "was it the card?"). Offered excuses get
accepted out of politeness and you learn nothing.

If the card wall came up, ask what they thought it was going to cost. **Their answer tells you
whether the disclosure reads the way we think it does** — which is now a live question, since the
signup page was quoting the wrong plan's price until 2026-08-29.

*Log: `Notes`, and any verbatim.*

### 5. What software they pay for

> "Totally separate question — what tools do you pay for each month? Any kind, doesn't have to be
> finance."

Software only. **Not what they invest, not their account size** — the field in the log is
deliberately *"price they pay or have paid for software"*, and the boundary is the compliance rule,
not a style preference.

This calibrates $9.99 / $19.99 against what they already accept, without asking a price question
they will answer aspirationally.

*Log: `Pays for (software)`.*

### 6. The Sean Ellis question

> "If Tapeline disappeared tomorrow, what would you use instead — and would you actually mind?"

The second clause is the whole question. "I'd go back to Finviz and honestly it'd be fine" and
"I'd have to rebuild my sheet, that'd be annoying" are the difference between a nice-to-have and a
product.

*Log: `What they'd be disappointed to lose`.*

---

## Closing (30 seconds)

> "That's everything. Last thing — is there anything I should have asked and didn't?"

The best single question on the call, and the one most often skipped. Then: no pitch, no upgrade
ask, no "so would you consider Premium?" You asked for their time to learn, and turning it into a
sale at the end is how you stop getting second interviews.

---

## Immediately after — while it is still fresh

Log it in `FEEDBACK_LOG.md` **within the hour**. Memory of a 15-minute call decays fast and the
verbatims are the first thing to go.

**Verbatims are the deliverable.** Write down the exact phrases, not your paraphrase. Landing-page
copy that works is almost always something a customer said first — and Tapeline currently has a
message-test problem that paid traffic cannot answer at its budget, so the copy has to come from
somewhere. This is that somewhere.

Update the running tallies at the top of the log, including **"times the same alternative was
named"**. Three people naming the same tool is a positioning fact; three people naming three tools
means you do not have a segment yet.

---

## What "done" looks like

Six entries with all fields filled. Not six conversations — six *recorded* ones. `OPERATING_RULES.md`
counts the log, not the calls.

Two things to expect and not flinch at:

- **Most will say the alternative was free.** Finviz free tier and a spreadsheet are the real
  incumbents. That is a pricing and positioning fact, not a failure.
- **Some will not remember signing up.** That is a real datum about the acquisition channel, and it
  belongs in the log rather than being smoothed over.

If six interviews say the same thing and it contradicts the current positioning, the positioning
changes. That is what the gate is for. `OPERATING_RULES.md` §9 is explicit: a rule about to be
broken gets rewritten in that file first, with a dated reason — never silently ignored.
