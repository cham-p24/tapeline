# Meta ads — execution playbook

*Written 2026-08-22. Extends `META_GO_LIVE.md` (the operator runbook: blockers, geo, event choice, kill criteria — all of which stand unchanged) with the execution depth underneath it: what the paid-course / agency / creative-system corpus actually teaches, verified, and cut down to what survives three filters at once — the FPS Special Ad Category, the AFSL descriptive-only copy posture, and A$10–50/day. Companion to `META_ADS_DECISION.md`, which is the analysis and still recommends **against** running Meta at all; nothing here reopens that verdict. The founder's decision to run it anyway as a bounded message test (2026-08-21) is taken as given, and this document exists so that test is run the way the best available evidence says to run it. Built from an eight-sweep research pass (YouTube practitioners, freed paid-course material, SaaS-specialist agencies, fintech case studies, creative systems, small-budget mechanics, funnel patterns, measurement) fact-checked through three verification lenses; every kill and correction from those lenses is applied below, and claims the lenses killed appear only as explicit warnings. Where this document and the gurus disagree, it is because the gurus assume budgets, targeting levers, or a legal register Tapeline does not have. Nothing here is legal advice. Evidence grades as in `META_ADS_DECISION.md`: A controlled/replicated · B large independent dataset · V vendor telemetry · C practitioner consensus · D single case · E folklore. USD unless marked A$.*

## 1. What the paid courses actually teach — the load-bearing ideas

Eight ideas carry essentially all of the 2025–26 corpus. Each is graded and marked for whether it survives FPS + Tapeline's constraints.

| # | Idea | Who teaches it · grade | Survives FPS + Tapeline? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Creative is the targeting."** Post-Andromeda, Meta reads the ad itself to decide who sees it; broad + self-selecting creative now outperforms narrow audience construction, so narrow interest targeting was already losing before FPS removed it. An FPS advertiser in 2026 gives up less than 2020-era folklore implies — the one lever that still hurts to lose is lookalikes seeded from payers. | Stackmatix, AdRiseLab, WOLF Financial, Foxwell, Meta's Andromeda docs · C (mechanism V) | **Yes — it is the entire plan.** FPS forces what the consensus now recommends anyway. |
| 2 | **Distinct concepts, not variations.** Andromeda's retrieval stage collapses near-duplicate ads into one delivery entity: 6 crops of the same screenshot buy one lottery ticket, not six. Diversity that counts = different visual concept + different message + different intended persona. Meta's own test: 1 ad set × 25 distinct creatives beat 5 × 5 by +17 % conversions at −16 % cost. | Jon Loomer, CTC, Segwise, TheOptimizer · C; Meta internal test, known only via AdScale’s retelling · C (not V — secondhand vendor claim) | **Yes, budget-scaled.** The "8–20 live ads" volume floors are for scaled accounts; at this budget the ceiling is **3–5 genuinely distinct concepts** (§3), or several arms never clear 1,000 impressions. |
| 3 | **Consolidate below ~50 events/week: one campaign, one ad set.** Fragmentation is the #1 documented structural killer of small accounts — every split divides event volume and no cell ever becomes readable. Meta's own Performance 5 framework says the same from the vendor side (account simplification is pillar one). | Jon Loomer · C; Meta Performance 5 · V | **Yes, unchanged.** With one ad set the CBO-vs-ABO debate dissolves. |
| 4 | **Optimise the deepest event that can plausibly generate volume — and never retreat to clicks or landing-page views.** Click/LPV optimisation routes delivery to accidental-click and Audience Network junk (Loomer's split tests). Purchase is doubly wrong here: scarce, and ~14 days post-click — outside the 7-day click window by construction. | TripleDart, Grol Media, Flighted · C; Loomer split tests · C | **Yes.** `CompleteRegistration` — exactly what `META_GO_LIVE.md` §3 already chose. Post-card-gate a registration is a card-adjacent commitment signal, which strengthens the choice. |
| 5 | **Highest Volume bidding on an account with thin conversion history.** Cost controls need Meta to predict conversion probability from the account's own data; on a fresh account they choke delivery to zero. Near-unanimous: run Highest Volume until 30–100 weekly conversions establish a CPA baseline. | TheOptimizer, Flighted, Triple Whale, Pixamp · C | **Yes.** See the boxed warning in §3 for the commonly-taught cost-cap version of this test, which was reviewed and rejected. |
| 6 | **Lo-fi, platform-native creative beats polish — and the offer beats targeting, creative and structure.** 42 % of top-spending ads in Motion's 550k-ad dataset were lo-fi (phone-shot, minimal editing). Heath's #1 lever is the offer. Barry Hott: first-frame branding triggers learned ad-avoidance. | Motion 2026 benchmarks · B; Ben Heath · C; Barry Hott · C | **Yes, selectively.** Founder-producible statics and phone video: yes. The UGC-testimonial branch: **no** — the raw-UGC finding is a DTC result that inverts for credibility-driven finance (C), Tapeline has zero customers to quote, and a paid testimonial about a stock-scoring product reads as an implied performance endorsement under both the AFSL posture and Meta finance review. The honest "offer" translation: the public record and the $0-today card trial — never "free account, no card", which is false since #548. |
| 7 | **Expect ~92–96 % of ads to fail; judge the batch, not the ad.** Motion: 5–8 % of 550k ads became real winners; about half never got meaningful spend. CTC: the top 3.5 % of ads earn 66 % of total spend; ~29 launches to find one outlier; their median outlier takes 39 days to surface. | Motion 2026 benchmarks · B; CTC 170+ brands · B | **Yes — it reframes the verdict.** 3–5 concepts producing zero winners is the base-rate outcome, not proof the channel or the product failed. The test ranks messages against each other; it cannot prove Meta "doesn't work" (§7, §9). |
| 8 | **Concentrate spend into short bursts — a long dribble produces noise, not learning.** Foxwell: fixed budget over two weeks beats the same budget over six. Counterweight: new ad accounts carry an automatic daily spending limit (~$25–50/day, loosening over 30–90 days of clean billing), so a founder account cannot burst its way to a faster verdict — and budget spikes are themselves a new-account flag pattern. | Foxwell · C; Loomer / LeadEnforce on daily limits · C/E | **Yes, in bounded form.** A$30–50/day for a 3-week burst beats A$10–15/day for six weeks; the spending limit and account-warming both say hold it flat (§3, §5, §8). |

## 2. Where the gurus disagree — and which side the evidence favours

| Dispute | The camps | Which side, for this account |
|---|---|---|
| **Creative volume** | Loomer: top advertisers run 15–50 conceptually different ads and judge the aggregate. Motion/HexClad: cut 75 ads to 6 genuinely distinct. Theriot: 9–12 ad sets, one concept each. TheOptimizer: 8–12 concepts/month at $5–15k spend. | The only cross-camp consensus is *genuinely distinct*. Every volume prescription is budget-scaled: TheOptimizer's own evaluation floor (~$100–150 and 1,000–3,000 impressions per creative before judging, C) makes 3–5 concepts the honest maximum at A$10–50/day. More concepts guarantees unreadable arms, not more learning. |
| **Where tests live** | Heath: a dedicated ~20 % testing campaign feeding a scaling campaign. Theriot: abandoned separate testing campaigns — new concepts launch as ad sets inside the one CBO so they inherit conversion history. | Both camps agree on everything else (broad, direct-to-offer, creative is the lever). At this budget the disagreement resolves itself: two campaigns fragment an already-thin signal, so the Theriot *shape* wins — one campaign — collapsed further than Theriot himself runs it (his 9–12 ad sets at $5–10/day minimums is $45–120/day, unaffordable here): **one campaign, one ad set, 3–5 concepts inside it** (Foxwell's exact launch prescription for new accounts, C). |
| **Bidding** | Highest Volume consensus vs Charley Tichenor's cost-cap-first doctrine. | Highest Volume, full stop (§1 #5). Tichenor is the documented outlier, and even his material concedes caps suit scale-stage accounts. Note the arithmetic premise honestly: at US$20/day, $2–6 finance CPCs buy 3–7 clicks/day, and at the corpus’s own cold-social landing CVR of 2.5–4 % (TripleDart · C) that is ≈ **1–2 registrations/week** — not the 15–50/week sometimes assumed — which moves cost caps even further out of reach, makes Learning Limited the permanent expected state, and lengthens the honest per-concept evaluation window. |
| **The 3:2:2 method** | Still taught everywhere (3 concepts × 2 primary texts × 2 headlines in one Dynamic Creative ad set). | **Mechanically dead.** Dynamic Creative was deprecated in 2025; its successor Flexible Format was folded into an Advantage+ creative "flexible media" toggle in March 2026 (Metricool, Madgicx, Campaign Builder · C). Anyone describing the literal DCT setup is describing UI that no longer exists. The durable core survives: more distinct concepts than copy variants, delivery assembles combinations — **so every text must be lint-clean against every visual it could pair with, before launch.** |
| **Refresh cadence** | Weekly creative refresh (assumes $5k+/mo where frequency accumulates fast) vs frequency-triggered refresh. | Frequency-triggered. At founder spend against a US-wide forced-broad audience, fatigue arrives on a 4–8-week cycle, not weekly (Logical Position, Adamigo · C). Refresh cue: **frequency > 2.5–3.0**; cadence: 1–2 new concepts per month, not a rebuild. Do not burn scarce production on someone else's refresh schedule. |
| **Retargeting-only as the cheap play** | Grol Media: retargeting-only is "the single most cost-effective way" for small SaaS (TripleDart: retargeting CPL −45 % vs cold). | **Dead here, twice over:** tapeline.io traffic is below the ~1k-monthly-visitor pool the finding itself names as minimum viable, and a retargeting-only campaign cannot answer the test's actual question — which *message* moves strangers. The surviving fragment: the cold test builds the website-visitor pixel pool (FPS-legal) as a free byproduct for a possible later phase. |
| **One-variable-at-a-time testing** | Demand Curve's classic hygiene: change one component per iteration. | **Partially invalidated by Andromeda:** near-identical variants differing in one element get collapsed into one entity and never earn independent delivery, so single-variable A/Bs inside an ad set often can't produce a clean read. What survives: isolation *across* genuinely distinct concepts, and the cheap early kill signal — an ad whose CTR runs 30–40 % below its siblings after ~3,000 impressions (C). Demand Curve's ring-fenced 15 % experiment budget is incoherent here: 100 % of a message-test budget *is* the experiment. |

## 3. The campaign blueprint for this account

Everything in `META_GO_LIVE.md` §2 (blockers), §4 (geo), §5 (setup) and §7 (founder steps) stands. This section adds the settings depth and names the traps.

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Campaigns | **One.** Sales objective, conversions. | §1 #3. A second campaign at this budget is self-sabotage. |
| Ad sets | **One**, holding all concepts. | Event density. CBO vs ABO is moot with one ad set; ABO only matters if a second ad set ever exists (it guarantees each cell a floor instead of letting CBO starve one arm). |
| Special Ad Category | Financial Products & Services, declared. | Hard gate (`META_GO_LIVE.md` §2). Note the mechanics precisely: you do not "select broad targeting" — **FPS locks the audience** (fixed 18–65+, all genders, no lookalikes, no ZIP, no interest exclusions). Per the fact-checked table in `META_ADS_DECISION.md` §3: Advantage detailed-targeting expansion IS supported under FPS, approved-list interests may be available ([unverified] which survive), and custom audiences are allowed — dead here only because the payer list is empty and the site audience is a few hundred people, not because FPS forbids them. The one-CBO-broad systems the gurus teach are FPS-compatible *in effect*: the forced special-category audience is delivery-equivalent to the broad setup they describe. |
| Audience levers actually available | Location + language. Nothing else. | Heath's "location/age/language" loses the age lever under FPS. Detailed-targeting note for accuracy: the field was not removed platform-wide — exclusions went in stages through 2025, impacted interests stopped delivering 2026-01-15, and surviving selections are non-binding "audience suggestions" under Advantage+ delivery. None of that matters here: FPS campaigns don't get the field's remnants either, and every pre-2025 "interest-proxy workaround" for finance advertisers is dead advice. |
| Geo | US only; exclude AU, UK, TW, IN, SG, JP, IE, HK. | `META_GO_LIVE.md` §4 — hard legal gate, not a preference. |
| Placements | Advantage+ placements. | Hand-picking at this volume starves delivery. Instrument `{{placement}}` in UTMs (§7) and watch for Audience Network eating budget with junk traffic. |
| Optimisation event | `CompleteRegistration`. | `META_GO_LIVE.md` §3, now independently validated: the SaaS small-budget consensus is "optimise the aha-adjacent event, not Purchase, below ~20–30 paid customers from ads" (Grol · C), and never clicks/LPV (§1 #4). `StartTrial` and `Purchase` keep flowing via CAPI as measurement. |
| Bidding | **Highest Volume.** | §1 #5, §2. |
| Attribution | Leave the default; **judge on 7-day-click only** via Compare Attribution Settings (§7). | Early-2026 change: only link clicks count as "click-through" now; engagements + 5s video views moved to a 1-day "engage-through" bucket — the flimsiest evidence class. |
| Budget | A$30–50/day, held flat, 3-week burst (§8). Pre-set cap per `META_GO_LIVE.md` §5 ("whatever you are willing to lose"). | Arithmetic: judging 4 concepts needs ~US$100–150 each (C) ≈ **A$700–900 total**. At A$10/day the same test takes 6+ weeks and only 2 concepts are honestly readable — run fewer concepts, not thinner arms. The new-account daily limit (~$25–50/day) means the burst cannot be compressed by 5×-ing budget even if desired. |
| Advantage+ campaign types | **Skip.** Regular conversions campaign. | A+ Leads/Sales wants ~50+ conversions/week and $50–100/day; Logical Position's 2026 playbook explicitly carves out regulated industries for manual structure (C). Skipping is currently defensible, not laggard. |
| Automatic enhancements | **Off — every toggle that alters words** (text generation, overlays, "relevant comments"); re-check after each Ads Manager UI update because defaults reset. Flexible-media *ratio routing* (feed vs Reels asset selection) is fine. | Compliance-critical, not just performance (Foxwell · C): auto-generated text variations can inject urgency or recommendation language the AFSL linter never saw. Companion to `META_GO_LIVE.md` §2b's Automatic Advanced Matching = OFF. |
| Learning phase | **Plan to live in "Learning Limited" permanently.** | The 50-events/week exit needs $57–107/day at plausible registration costs. The penalty for never exiting is on the order of 5–10 % (C), not transformational. Do not restructure, switch events, or restart to chase the badge. |
| Edit discipline | Batch all changes into one weekly touch. Budget moves ≤ 20 % per step. Nothing judged inside 48 h. Never duplicate-and-relaunch. | Targeting / optimisation-event / bid-strategy changes reliably reset learning. Adding a creative to a running ad set often *no longer* resets it (documented 2025 behaviour change; Meta's docs are stale — treat a reset as possible, not certain). Each reset re-burns days of scarce data. |

**Commonly taught but wrong for this account — the killed and corrected doctrines, inline:**

- **Cost caps as the test design ("set a cap at your affordable cost-per-registration and let underspend be the kill signal") — reviewed and killed.** It sounds elegant, and CTC/Foxwell teach it for accounts with history. On a brand-new FPS account, a cap at an economics-derived affordable cost-per-registration — which §7 derives as (affordable cost-per-trial ~$13–33) × the observed registration→trial rate, i.e. strictly BELOW $13–33 and far below the plausible finance-vertical $20–60 — behaves like a bid cap and delivers nothing. (The $50 line in `META_GO_LIVE.md` §8 is a deliberately looser message-test tolerance, not the economics-derived cap; the two numbers answer different questions and neither belongs in the bidding field.) Zero spend then measures account immaturity, not message viability: the test produces no reads at all. Cost controls become a legitimate tool only after 30–100 weekly conversions establish a baseline, a state this account will not reach. Highest Volume, with kill criteria doing the protecting (§7).
- **A separate testing campaign** (Heath) — fragments the budget below readability. One campaign (§2).
- **9–12 ad sets / 5–6 new concepts weekly** (Theriot) — $45–120/day of ad-set minimums and ~5× founder creative capacity. Keep his principles (distinct concepts, 7-day patience, 80/20 iterate-winners-over-new-angles), collapse the structure.
- **"15–20+ live ads for Andromeda"** — a quota invented for scaled accounts; Loomer explicitly rebuts it (Andromeda is retrieval, not a quota; near-duplicate pumping is exactly what it does *not* reward). Precise-sounding numbers in this genre ("similarity > 60 % triggers suppression", "+65 % ROAS at 20+ ads/month") could not be traced to Meta — invented precision.
- **10 creator hooks per video / hired micro-influencers** (Heath) — produces 4–7× more ads than this budget can push past 1,000 impressions each, and paid creators fronting a stock-scoring product invite testimonial/endorsement exposure. Founder-shot substitutes (§4).
- **Lead magnets / Instant Forms for cold traffic** — direct product offers out-qualify gated content 3–5× on paid social (Gelb · C); lead-ads SQL conversion runs 35–55 % lower (GrowthSpree · B). Tapeline's lead-magnet equivalent is the public record — as a *landing destination*, not a gated email capture. (Instant Forms re-enter only if a newsletter play is ever run, §6.)
- **Optimising Landing Page Views or Link Clicks "to get volume"** — the quality-collapse trap (Loomer's split tests). Registration or nothing.
- **Retargeting-only; interest workarounds; lookalikes from payers; customer-list uploads** — dead for this account (§2; FPS; a 0-payer list; the March-2025 US financial customer-list restriction). The message test runs on **one lever: forced-broad delivery steered by self-selecting creative**, while the pixel quietly accumulates a site-visitor pool for later. Complete the one-time anti-discrimination data certification in Ads Manager anyway, so future list uploads aren't blocked.

## 4. Creative system

### 4.1 Formats that work with descriptive-only finance copy

Ranked for this account. Every format shows the real product UI or plain text — no stock imagery, no AI-generated faces or implied people (AI faces cost conversions on considered purchases: −8 % CVR over $100, −14 % over $500, Columbia/Harvard/CMU × Taboola · B — and hand a skeptical finance prospect a reason to distrust the record). Normalise a standing footer on every asset — *"Informational only. Descriptive scores, not recommendations."* — the way Robinhood carries its risk line as standard furniture (Motion Ad Library teardown · B).

| Priority | Format | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Text-led static** ("one core idea" / anti-ad plain-text) | Statics validate a message for ~5 % of video's cost in ~72 h of spend (Pigeon Digital · C); cold-audience statics need a message, not a product shot; Tapeline's pitch is *literally* the anti-ad format. The 20 %-text-overlay penalty died in 2020 — text-heavy statics carry no delivery cost. Motley Fool, the closest business-model analog, runs 68 % text-led headline ads (Motion · B). | This is the launch batch. Longer primary text is an underused arbitrage on Facebook specifically (Metadata $15M analysis · B, pre-Andromeda — trust the direction). |
| 2 | **Scanner screen-recording, 15–30 s** | The mechanic-as-hook pattern is the highest-share format at the closest big-budget peer (Acorns: app screen-recordings 31 % of actives, Motion · B) and is inherently descriptive: "this is what it shows", never "this is what you should do". A concrete number in the first 3 seconds lifts finance hook rate ~31 % (Meta 2025 FS benchmark via Adamigo · C) — use descriptive numbers only: one number 0–100, the 60-second refresh, days tracked. | Screen + voiceover, phone-recorded. No hype needed: the score appearing *is* the hook. |
| 3 | **Scorecard-artifact static** | The eToro legitimacy play at founder scale: finance buyers respond to institutional-proof artifacts they can check themselves (Motion · B). Tapeline's artifact is the public record. **Correction applied: show the page's existence and completeness in the creative; keep hit-rate / alpha numerals off the ad unit** — in-ad win-rate figures are the finance-review flag class and banned from acquisition copy by the house rules anyway. The landing page (which Meta crawls and rewards for transparency) carries the numbers. | A screenshot of the `/scorecard` per-day entries table, cropped to show the misses and to EXCLUDE the summary-stat strip (hit-rate / alpha numerals) — the page’s own H1/OG deliberately carry no percentages, and the ad unit matches that discipline. The landing page carries the numbers; the ad carries the existence of the record. |
| 4 | **Founder-to-camera, 30–60 s, phone-shot** | Highest-consensus B2B/SaaS format (~40 % more engagement than polished motion graphics · C); captures UGC's authenticity without the testimonial exposure; inherently compatible with a calm factual voice. Hott's rules: no logo/brand font in the first frame, natural light, real background. | **Graduate winning static messages into this — don't test with it.** One statistics-style founder ad is allowed: built from non-performance facts (days tracked, tickers covered, refresh cadence, total scores logged) — never hit rate. Press-screenshot proof ads are unusable pre-launch (no press), testimonial ads are impossible at 0 customers and a compliance minefield besides. |
| 5 | **Carousel (problem → mechanism → proof-artifact → public-pages CTA)** | The B2B explainer format, ~2× CTR claims are agency-asserted (C/D) but it maps cleanly: "500 filters" / "one number" / "the record is public" / "reading it needs no account". Strongest in retargeting. | Hold for a later phase once the pixel pool exists. |

**Asset kit per concept:** one 1:1 or 4:5 for feed + one 9:16 for Reels/Stories built to the unified safe zone (text out of the top ~14 %, bottom ~20–35 %, sides 6 % — Meta consolidated all four vertical placements into one 9:16 zone in March 2026, with a Safe Zone Guardrail overlay in Ads Manager · V/C). Don't force statics into 9:16 — Reels is where video earns its reach.

### 4.2 Hook patterns that need no hype

Four of Motion's five winning hook archetypes survive the AFSL linter; the urgency/offer class is forfeited — **and nothing real is lost, because Meta's finance review rejects that class too.** The rejection layer bans what the linter bans (return promises, urgency around financial decisions, guaranteed outcomes, wealth imagery): descriptive-only copy is a policy *advantage* — fewer rejections, no account-quality damage — while hype-dependent competitors get filtered (benly, roaspig · C; `META_GO_LIVE.md` §6 said this first).

| Archetype (Motion · B) | Compliant Tapeline translation |
|---|---|
| Confession | "I built this because my own weekend screening process was fourteen browser tabs." |
| Relatability | "Sunday night. Fourteen tabs of stock screeners. Again." |
| Contrast | "500 filters vs one number." |
| Curiosity | "We publish the scorecard, misses included. The log is one click away." |
| Bold claim | Verifiable fact only, never a performance promise: "The scorecard has been public since day one — misses included." |

Two hard rules govern every hook:

1. **The hook does the targeting** (FPS survival consensus · C): the first line names the person or the workflow so the wrong people scroll past. Map **each concept to exactly one persona** (Denney: Andromeda clusters on persona; two concepts aimed at the same persona in one round make the message read ambiguous · C). Personas for round one: the spreadsheet-DIY screener, the skeptic who wants receipts, the 20-minute evening reviewer, the burned-by-hype refugee.
2. **Qualify by workflow, never by financial situation.** Second-person financial-state copy ("Struggling with debt?", "Want to grow your savings?", "your portfolio is underperforming") is the #1 documented finance rejection trigger — Meta's "personal attributes" rule (Overt Digital, Digital Eagle · C). "If you screen 500 tickers by hand every weekend…" is legal targeting; "if your returns disappoint…" is a rejection. **Linter gap, flagged:** `scripts/lint-copy-compliance.mjs` blocks promises and urgency but not second-person financial-state phrasing — worth adding a rule before ad-copy volume grows.

Also banned in every creative and comment reply, from Meta's written policy (V): any "DM us" / "message for details" invitation — US-targeted investment-adjacent ads may not suggest direct-message interaction (the anti-pig-butchering rule). If the inbox auto-handler is ever extended to Facebook comments, it must never invite DMs on ad posts.

### 4.3 Testing cadence, honest about the budget

- **Launch the full batch at once** (3–5 concepts, submitted as paused ads days early — §5), then let delivery run. Expect Meta to concentrate 60–80 % of spend on an early favourite within days: **that concentration is a test result** (revealed preference), not a malfunction to fight with forced even splits.
- **Statics first; video second.** Validate which *message* wins on text-led statics, then spend founder-video effort only on the 1–2 surviving angles.
- **Reads, in order of when they stabilise:** hook rate for video (3-sec views / impressions; Motion's 550k-ad benchmarks · B: cold-feed typical 18–28 %, median ~22–23 %, <18 % = first-frame failure, Reels 24–36 % — use these as the floor and STILL rank Tapeline's ads against each other) → sibling-relative CTR at ~3,000 impressions (kill an ad running 30–40 % under its siblings) → cost per `CompleteRegistration` on 7-day click (§7). When a video is weak, fix in order: first frame → on-screen text → product visibility, before rewriting the ad (Motion · C).
- **Iteration rule once a winner exists:** next burst = fresh executions of the winning angle competing against each other **in the same campaign** — never a new campaign (resets delivery history), never pausing a proven performer to make room, and never judging a new ad against a historically-optimised winner (an unfair fight the new ad always loses; Motion/CTC · C). Theriot's 80/20: mostly iterate winners, always keep ~1 genuinely new angle in play.
- **Refresh trigger:** frequency > 2.5–3.0, not the calendar. Add 1–2 new concepts monthly. Reserve ~10–15 % of the total budget for slower-burn concepts — CTC's median outlier surfaced at day 39 (B).

### 4.4 Copy bank — eight new variants, extending `META_ADS_DECISION.md` §9

*Same house rules as the §9 bank: descriptive only; no exact weights; no win-rate numbers in acquisition copy; the record is a character claim, not a benefit headline. Two corrections from the verification lenses are applied throughout and bind all future edits: (a) no "picks" token in ad copy — Meta's finance classifier pattern-matches it to stock-tip services, so it is "score(s)" everywhere; (b) no hit-rate / alpha numerals in the ad unit. Since any primary text can pair with any headline inside a concept's flexible set, every combination below was linted, not just the pairs as printed.*

**Stale-entry warning before reuse:** §9's variant 2 ("See what the scanner read — free" / "…the top of the ranked list, live, no card") was written 2026-08-20 and **falsified by the card gate two days later** (#548: new accounts must put a card on file at `/app/start` before using the logged-in product). Do not run it as written — and note `META_GO_LIVE.md` §6’s creative table still carried that headline verbatim (corrected in the same PR as this playbook). Corrected form: headline "See what the scanner read — no account needed", body pointing at the public pages ("The scorecard and ticker pages are public — reading them needs no account and no card."). Note also that CI never lints `docs/**` (`META_GO_LIVE.md` §6) — **manually re-run `node scripts/lint-copy-compliance.mjs` over this file after any copy edit.** (Same staleness class, flagged in passing: `PAID_MARKETING_PLAYBOOK.md` §5.3's Google RSA lines "Free Tier, 24h Delayed" and the "Free tier, no card" callout predate both the 2026-06-20 freemium retune and #548 — fix before that build is ever enabled.)

6. **Persona: the spreadsheet-DIY screener.** Format: anti-ad plain-text static. Land: `/signup?from=screener`.
   "If your process is fourteen browser tabs and a hand-built spreadsheet every Sunday night, Tapeline does the compression for you: one 0–100 composite score and one plain sentence per ticker — the scanner scores every name in its universe, refreshed through the session. You still make every decision — the scanner just shortens the reading."
   Headline: **"Retire the Sunday-night spreadsheet."**

7. **Persona: the skeptic who wants receipts.** Format: scorecard-artifact static. Land: `/scorecard` (public, no account).
   "Every score Tapeline has published is logged — the misses too, nothing deleted. Summary stats are live for everyone; day-by-day entries go public on a 7-day delay. The record exports as CSV, and reading it needs no account."
   Headline: **"The whole record is one click away."**

8. **Persona: the burned-by-hype refugee.** Format: text-led static; later a founder-video script seed. Land: `/scorecard`.
   "A lot of market content shows you only its highlights. Every score Tapeline has published is logged in one public table — summary stats live, per-day entries public on a 7-day delay — so you can check what its scores said against what happened next. Descriptive labels, never instructions."
   Headline: **"We publish the log, not the highlights."**

9. **Persona: the trial-hesitant (card objection).** Format: plain-text static. Land: `/signup?from=trial`.

## 5. New-account survival

The finance vertical sits next to Meta's biggest scam category, and the documented failure mode is a cascade: automated rejection → advertiser edits slightly and resubmits → rejections accumulate on a young account → restriction → permanent disable, with post-disable appeals rarely succeeding (Meta "Unacceptable Business Practices" standard · V; community disable threads · C/D). Everything below is cheap insurance against that cascade; some of it is graded folklore (E) and worth doing anyway because the downside is losing the account, not an ad.

1. **Before anything spends:** Business Manager confirmed, domain verified, 2FA on, a payment card that clears (payment failure is a top new-account kill), and a backup admin + separate BM access pre-built — finance accounts are disabled without warning. The Facebook page should have some age and content, not be born the same day as the BM, pixel, and first campaign (E).
2. **Submit the entire first batch as paused ads, days early.** New accounts in sensitive verticals get slower review (hours to 24–48 h) and more aggressive false rejections; each clean approval banks trust that speeds later reviews. Let the batch clear, then activate — decoupling review risk from the test timeline (§8). Keep one deliberately boring, approved "anchor" ad in the account permanently.
3. **On a wrongful rejection: appeal once through the official flow. Never resubmit edited variants** — that is the cascade's first step. Tapeline's register helps here: the claim-lightest, most descriptive copy in the bank is exactly what the classifier tolerates, so run the plainest ads in weeks 1–2 (E/C).
4. **Spending posture:** start at the low end, raise ≤ 20–30 % every 2–3 days at most, never yo-yo. The automatic daily spending limit (~$25–50/day on new accounts, loosening over 30–90 days of clean billing · C/E) sits inside the planned budget — the test itself is the account-warming.
5. **FPS declaration timing:** declared on the first campaign, before review (`META_GO_LIVE.md` §2 blocker 3). An undeclared-then-caught financial ad is a rejection on the record for no reason.
6. **Two silent classifications to monitor, both with named responses:**
   - **Domain-level event restriction.** Meta blocks standard events (Purchase, Lead, CompleteRegistration) at the *domain* level for domains it classifies under financial-services data-sharing restrictions — silently, pixel and CAPI alike. Tapeline read as exempt — "Core setup: Off" — in Events Manager → dataset "Tapeline" (28351455154543230) → Settings, read 2026-08-22 immediately after pixel creation, before any events had been sent. `META_ADS_DECISION.md` §3 expected this read to need event flow first; it did not — the settings page exposes it on a fresh dataset. Take the authoritative re-read at GO_LIVE §7 step 8 once events flow, and record both. **Re-check Events Manager after every campaign edit**; the failure mode is silent optimisation degradation, not an error. If it flips: stop — that is `META_GO_LIVE.md` §8's first kill criterion.
   - **Regulator-verification creep.** Meta's financial-advertiser verification regime (prove authorisation — AFSL in AU, FCA in UK) expanded to India in July 2025 and reportedly toward ~38 countries through 2026; one Business Help Centre list already names the United States (V/C, partially unverified). **Named kill criterion: if US verification for investment products arrives, this channel closes for an unlicensed publisher-exemption product.** Screenshot the current exemption state before spending, and re-check monthly.
7. **The landing page is part of review.** Meta crawls it: complete product description, all fees, key terms, and privacy policy visible without hunting; the ad's promise restated so there is no ad-to-page expectation mismatch (a documented rejection trigger). This is the same checklist Google §5.5 already imposes — one page can satisfy both.

## 6. Funnel

**The evidence supports direct-to-offer, with the offer honestly stated — and Tapeline has exactly two honest cold offers:**

1. **The public record** — `/scorecard` and the ticker pages, readable with no account and no card. This is simultaneously: the FPS-era "educational resource" offer that finance practitioners converged on post-2025 (Lone Beacon · C — educational framing also sidesteps the ad-authorisation trigger in Meta's own policy · V); the trust layer whose absence is why bootstrapper cold-social attempts fail (Indie Hackers base rate · C); and the retargeting-pool builder FPS still allows. It behaves like a content offer, not a freemium signup.
2. **The card-required trial, sold on its transparency** — $0 today, exact first-charge date, one-click cancel (variant 9). Card-required trials net ~3× the payers per 1,000 visitors despite 40–60 % fewer signups (ChartMogul/ProductLed · B; GrowthSpree · B). Two planning consequences: registration volume will read "bad" against freemium benchmarks — *that is the gate, not the creative* — and each registration is worth ~3× a freemium registration downstream, which is priced into the kill thresholds (§7).

What the evidence rejects: lead magnets and gated captures for cold traffic (3–5× worse downstream quality · C/B, §3), and any ad implying a card-free account — false since #548. Disclosing the price and the card requirement **in the ad** is the correct trade: it costs CTR and buys card-wall completion, and you pay per click while optimising registrations (WOLF, self-selection consensus · C).

**Destination policy:** message match decides per variant, and the destination is frozen for the whole burst — swapping mid-test is both a significant edit and a change to the measured funnel. Record-persona ads land on the record; product-mechanic ads land on `/signup?from=`. Landing-page conversion moves cost-per-registration more than anything left in Ads Manager (identical media at 2 % vs 5 % page conversion is a 2.5× CAC difference — WOLF · C), and message match is the cheapest large lever there is (66 % lift in the classic single case · D; principle universal · C): **if the ad's promise isn't restated in the landing H1, the test measures the page's translation, not the message.** The `?from=` mechanism exists (`META_GO_LIVE.md` §6); new keys needed below are a small frontend addition — ship them before launch or fold those variants into an existing key.

| Variant | Destination | H1 echo required | `from` key |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 "Retire the Sunday-night spreadsheet." | `/signup?from=screener` | exists (screener framing) | existing |
| 7 "The whole record is one click away." | `/scorecard` | page *is* the promise | n/a (public page) |
| 8 "We publish the log, not the highlights." | `/scorecard` | page is the promise | n/a |

Two funnel caveats carried from the wider corpus: the B2B Meta journey is structurally two-step anyway — ad view → content visit → branded-search return days later (Scale Growth · C) — so sending cold finance traffic to proof content is a recognised pattern, not a compromise; and **the open-access month makes "upgrade to unlock the full scanner" false in any copy until 2026-09-08** (`tier.py:PROMO_OPEN_ACCESS_UNTIL`) — none of the bank's variants reference scanner-row gating, and variants 6/11 use SYSTEM framing (“the scanner scores every name”) rather than view-entitlement framing (“you see every name”) — which keeps every ad true on both sides of the revert. The newsletter-first funnel (McGarry $1–3/sub) stays on the shelf: its benchmarks come from non-FPS accounts with targeting levers Tapeline cannot touch; FPS-adjusted expectation is nearer $2–6+/sub (correction applied), and it only beats direct if the daily content is top-decile (beehiiv: median finance free→paid 0.78 %, top decile 20 % — a 25× spread · B). A content bet, not a media-buying bet.

## 7. Measurement at this scale

**The stance, from the strongest evidence in the entire corpus:** Ads Manager's attributed conversions are correlational, not causal. Across 663 Facebook RCTs, the best observational methods — with 5,000+ user features — overestimated true lift by 3–12×, worst for lower-funnel events like registrations (Gordon et al., *Marketing Science* 2023 · A; the 2019 15-experiment study found the same · A). `META_ADS_DECISION.md` §2 built its verdict on this; the message test inherits it: in-platform numbers rank creatives against each other and nothing more.

**Read weekly, one sitting, same day each week:**

| Read | Where | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Blended cost per new signup = spend ÷ (new accounts − pre-ads baseline/day) | Own DB + ad invoice | The only unfakeable number (MER logic · C). Kill criteria are written against this and the per-ad registration cost, nothing else. |
| Cost per `CompleteRegistration`, **7-day-click column only** (Compare Attribution Settings; "First conversion" metric) | Ads Manager | Blended headline numbers mix windows; view-through and the new 1-day "engage-through" bucket (2026 redefinition) are the flimsiest evidence and structurally contaminated by organic/AEO exposure. |
| Hook rate + sibling-relative CTR per ad | Ads Manager | The metrics that stabilise first at this volume (§4.3). |
| Registration → card-wall completion → `StartTrial` | Own DB | The junk-quality tripwire: if Meta finds a pocket of worthless registrants, this chain shows it first (Loomer quality-trap logic · C). Post-#548 semantics: every NEW account meets the card wall at `/app/start` before using the product, so the first quality read is card-wall completion rate, with `StartTrial` behind it. **Day-1 prerequisite (do not skip): verify the full signup → card wall → StartTrial event chain fires end-to-end with a throwaway account and a Stripe test card BEFORE spending** — a signup flow with a new interstitial is exactly where a conversion event silently dies, and a dead event here means the campaign optimises on nothing. Directional only — expect single digits of StartTrial per burst, which is also why **no kill criterion sits directly on cost-per-StartTrial** (correction applied): chain the trial economics up-funnel instead — affordable cost-per-registration ≈ affordable cost-per-trial (~$13–33 at the CAC ceiling) × observed registration→trial rate — and put the kill line on registrations. |
| Frequency | Ads Manager | Refresh cue at 2.5–3.0 (§4.3). |
| Events Manager: domain-restriction status + dedupe diagnostics | Events Manager | §5.6; and dedupe failure double-counts registrations, silently halving apparent cost — a false pass of the kill criteria. Note the shipped architecture (`META_GO_LIVE.md` §2b): conversions are SERVER-ONLY — the browser pixel fires PageView and nothing else — so pixel/CAPI dedupe is N/A today. The check is simpler: each conversion appears exactly ONCE in Events Manager. Dedupe via shared `event_id` (already derived deterministically in `services/meta_capi.py`) becomes live only if a browser conversion event is ever added — which would also have to survive the §2b privacy scoping. |
| Spend pacing vs plan | Ads Manager | Underdelivery on a new account is §5's spending-limit mechanics, not a signal about the message. |

**Ignore entirely:** in-platform ROAS and the Purchase column (a day-14 trial conversion is outside every available click window, and web CAPI rejects events older than 7 days — the column reads ~0 regardless of truth · V; payers are counted only via the stored-identifier → Stripe join, per `META_ADS_DECISION.md` §5); daily wiggles (iOS numbers carry modelled components post-AEM; read weekly aggregates — and no AEM configuration exists to do anymore, the 8-event ranking was removed mid-2025 · C); the Learning Limited badge (§3).

**Attribution mechanics that change dates:** conversions report on the *click* date, so the last 7 days always look worse than they will end up. **No verdict on any window until 7+ days after it closes** — the burst's decision date is burst-end + 7 (§8).

**Attribution mechanics that change counts:** every Facebook click — paid or organic — arrives through the `l.facebook.com` link shim, so `signup_referrer_host` cannot split paid from organic; UTMs are the only paid marker, and in-app-browser / cross-device journeys drop them. Treat UTM-attributed signups as a **floor**. Wiring: UTMs in the ad-level URL Parameters field using dynamic IDs, not names (`utm_source=facebook&utm_medium=paid-social&utm_campaign={{campaign.id}}&utm_content={{ad.id}}&utm_term={{placement}}` — renames silently fork analytics); persist first-touch UTMs + click IDs on the User row at registration (the `signup_gclid` pattern already exists for Google). Cover the gap with **self-reported attribution**: one optional free-text "How did you hear about us?" field at signup — click tools undercount no-click channels 5–10× (C), and at this volume every answer is hand-readable. Ship it before launch.

**EMQ expectations with the current email + external_id-only matching:** expect a score around 5–6 — at the "good" floor, short of the 8+ that high-intent events want (CustomerLabs/Stape consensus · C). The cheapest meaningful upgrade is capturing `fbclid` into a first-party `_fbc` cookie at landing and forwarding it **unhashed** with `fbp` — never hash `fbc`/`fbp`/IP/user-agent (hashing silently zeroes their value). Registration events already possess the email at event time, so 8+ is reachable **without collecting anything new** — and should be: phone/DOB/address measurably raise EMQ but a scanner signup has no legitimate reason to hold them, and hashed-email sharing is already on the lawyer's desk (`META_GO_LIVE.md` §10 Q3–5). Same increment, applied to `Purchase`: ensure the Stripe-webhook CAPI event carries the fbp/fbc/email captured at registration (Stape pattern · C) — at this budget it buys truthful channel-level reads for the kill decision, not in-window optimisation.

**Incrementality, honestly:** proper geo-holdout or Conversion Lift testing starts around $30–50k/month — out of reach. The accessible substitute is the on/off pre/post read against Tapeline's own signup baseline, and the near-zero baseline is the rare case where pre/post is most credible: a handful of incremental signups/day is a visible step-change. Log confounds during the window (AEO spikes, launches, newsletter sends).

**Pre-registered verdict criteria — `META_GO_LIVE.md` §8, folded in and refined, written down before launch:**

1. **Stop immediately** if the domain shows as data-sharing restricted, or if US financial-advertiser verification arrives (§5.6). *(GO_LIVE §8 + this doc.)*
2. **Stop at the pre-set spend cap**, whatever happens. *(GO_LIVE §8.)*
3. **Stop** if cost per `CompleteRegistration` exceeds ~$50 after 100+ clicks. *(GO_LIVE §8 — and calibrated by the benchmarks: finance CPL runs ~$25–60 (WordStream · B), so a registration materially under ~$25–40 would itself be a notable positive.)*
4. **Per-ad refinements** (research floors, not competitors to the above): no per-ad verdict before ~1,000–2,000 impressions *and* 48 h *and* 7 days from launch or last significant edit; kill an ad at **3× the target cost-per-registration spent with zero registrations** (a structural signal — page mismatch, broken event, dead message); soft-review at 1.5–2× sustained 5+ days; sibling-CTR kill per §4.3. Compound thresholds — time *and* spend *and* events — never one alone (Neiman, Coinis, admanage · C). **Public-page arms are exempt from the registration-cost kill**: any variant landing on `/scorecard` or `/how-it-works` (7, 8, 12 in the bank) has no signup flow on its landing page, so "zero registrations at 3× spend" is its EXPECTED state, not a structural failure. Pre-register their reads separately — CTR, `/scorecard`-visit step-change vs the Day-1 baseline, self-reported-attribution mentions, branded-search lift — and rank them in the Day 28–30 read on those, not on `utm_content` registrations. If the whole burst must be rankable on one metric, route every arm to `/signup?from=` instead and drop the public-page variants from the paid test.
5. **The verdict date is burst-end + 7 days** (attribution backfill). Conclusions are drawn per concept-batch, not per ad.
6. **Success** = one message earns registrations materially cheaper than its siblings — even if every arm is unprofitable. The ranking is the deliverable and transfers to AEO copy, email subjects, and comparison-page H1s. *(GO_LIVE §8, unchanged.)*
7. **Pre-committed interpretations:** zero winners from 3–5 concepts is the base-rate outcome (5–10 % concept hit rate · B/C) and licenses "these messages didn't clear the bar", never "Meta is disproven" — and equally, a good small-scale number does **not** project: documented CAC roughly doubled within days of scaling in the closest founder case (D), so any CAC readout is a lower bound and re-opens nothing in `META_ADS_DECISION.md` §7's gate.

What the test cannot measure remains what `META_GO_LIVE.md` §9 said: trial→paid. The instrument for that is still interviews and the existing trialists, at $0.

## 9. The sceptic's box — the strongest evidence-backed case this still fails

Stated fairly, because the pre-registered criteria only work if this paragraph was read first.

- **The economics were never rescued.** ~$164/payer best case vs a ~$30–60 affordable CAC (`META_ADS_DECISION.md` §4) — and the closest published comp says that internal estimate is near the *optimistic* end: self-serve sub-$50/mo SaaS lands at $200–600 paid CAC on Meta (AdRiseLab · C), B2B SaaS Meta ROAS benchmarks at ~1.24, roughly break-even (Powered by Search · C).
- **The budget is 3–15× below every specialist's floor.** No SaaS agency claims conversion-optimised Meta works below ~$2k/month (TripleDart, Flighted, AdRiseLab, converging · C). The account will live in Learning Limited, permanently, by design.
- **The base rates say zero winners is the likely outcome.** 5–8 % of ads become winners (Motion · B); ~29 launches to find an outlier (CTC · B); this test funds 3–5.
- **The category's best-converting register is legally off the table.** The closest business-model analog (Motley Fool) wins on past-performance hype, urgency, and FOMO — all barred by the AFSL posture and the linter (Motion Ad Library · B). Expect structurally worse CTR than category benchmarks; the enforcement asymmetry (the Fool demonstrably runs language a young account could never risk) is real and unfair.
- **The category's famous cheap CACs came from referral loops, not paid.** Robinhood's SEC-disclosed $15–53 per funded account rode 80 %+ organic/referral acquisition (S-1 · B). The case studies validate Tapeline's already-built referral program, not this channel.
- **The structural analyses all point the same way.** Cohen's ARPU/25 rule prices the affordable click at ~$0.50 against $2–6 FPS reality (C); Shapiro's handbook channels low-ARPU broad software to content and PLG, not paid social (C); the Indie Hackers base rate for cold Facebook on sub-$30/mo SaaS with no social proof is failure (C); and Tapeline's own A$951 → 0 on Google is the house's contribution to that base rate.
- **Even success doesn't scale.** The one closely-matched founder case that worked at small spend saw CAC roughly double within days of scaling (D). A passed message test licenses a second message test, not a channel.

And the other side of the ledger, equally fairly: the cost is bounded by a pre-set cap; the deliverable — a ranked answer to "which honest, descriptive message makes a stranger act" — transfers at full value to the channels that already produce engaged users; the card gate makes each registration ~3× a freemium registration; descriptive copy is a measurable review advantage in this vertical rather than a tax; and the pixel pool, EMQ plumbing, and account trust built during the test are reusable if the `META_ADS_DECISION.md` §7 gate is ever legitimately met. That is what the tuition buys. Nothing more was ever claimed for it.
