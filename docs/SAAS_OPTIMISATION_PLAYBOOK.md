## 1. The model — how a SaaS compounds, on one page

### 1.1 The five stages and the loops between them

```
                 +---------------------- REFERRAL ----------------------+
                 |  (users, artifacts, pages, mentions recruit users)   |
                 v                                                      |
  ACQUISITION -> ACTIVATION -> RETENTION -> REVENUE --------------------+
  (visitor ->     (signup ->    (returns at   (pays; stays paying;
   signup)         first value)  natural       expands)
                                 frequency)
       ^              |              |             |
       |              |              |             +- funds acquisition (paid) only when LTV:CAC clears
       |              |              +--------------- the CEILING: plateau = new/month / churn
       |              +------------------------------ the MULTIPLIER: everything downstream is conditional on it
       +--------------------------------------------- the SLOPE: how fast the bucket fills
```

**AARRR / Pirate Metrics** (Dave McClure, 2007 *(older)*, grade C) gives the five stages. Three later frameworks say how they interact:

- **Leaky bucket first** (David Skok, *SaaS Metrics 2.0*, 2012–13 *(older)*, C; Brian Balfour, *Why Retention Is The Silent Killer* and *Growth Is Good, But Retention Is Forever*, Reforge, C; Andrew Chen, C): retention is the *ceiling* — steady-state subscribers ≈ new-per-month ÷ monthly churn — and virality is a *multiplier on* retention, not a substitute for it. Fill the bucket only after it holds water. The arithmetic below uses Tapeline's ~$9.60/mo gross profit per payer from `PAID_ADS_PATHWAY.md` (which assumes 5–10%/mo churn):

  | Monthly logo churn | 12-mo survival | Expected lifetime | LTV | Plateau at +10 payers/mo |
  |---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
  | 3% | 69% | 33 mo | $320 | 333 |
  | 5% | 54% | 20 mo | $192 | 200 |
  | 7% | 42% | 14 mo | $137 | 143 |
  | 10% | 28% | 10 mo | $96 | 100 |

  Two churn points are a 67% swing in LTV and in the plateau, and flip `PAID_ADS_PATHWAY.md`'s best-case LTV:CAC from 1.85:1 (at 5%) to ~3.1:1 (at 3%). Retention is not a post-PMF concern; it decides whether paid acquisition ever becomes legal to run. *(The table's precision matters at P2; the shape of the argument matters now.)*

- **Growth loops, not funnels** (Balfour, Winters & Kwok, *Growth Loops are the New Funnels*, Reforge 2018, C; Balfour's *Four Fits* / *Channel-Model Fit*, C): a funnel is linear and needs constant top-up; a loop reinvests an output (a user, a page, an artifact, a citation) as the next input. Companies compound on one or two loops, not ten tactics. Your *model* (ARPU) picks your channel: at $10–20/mo you are on the far left of Balfour's spectrum — virality, UGC/programmatic SEO, free artifacts, organic loops. Anything with a human in the loop (sales, cold outreach, sponsorships) is out until ARPU or volume changes.

- **The stair-step** (Rob Walling, *Stair Step Approach* 2015 *(older)* → *The SaaS Playbook* 2023 and later podcast talks, C): a bootstrapper climbs from a one-time product with one channel, to a recurring product with one channel that works, to a real SaaS — one channel at a time, worked until it clearly fails; marketing skills before product skills before engineering. At $20 ARPA "you might have five options" for channels; PPC and cold outreach fall out first.

### 1.2 The order of operations

1. **Prove the product is correct on the thing the ICP uses it for**, and that the value moment exists — activation (a signup reaches first value) and early return (an activated user comes back at the product's natural frequency). These are the only two conversion metrics that carry information at n<100 because they resolve in days, not months. (The first clause is new here: #507 showed a scanner can be flawless in code and still rank names the ICP does not trade because the data feed does not cover the liquid ones.)
2. **Prove people will pay for it** — the first ten organic payers, retained ≥60 days. A count, not a rate (0/5 is unreadable).
3. **Prove the bucket holds** — a cohort curve that flattens above zero; monthly logo churn ≤5% (≤3% with an annual mix) for a $10–20 prosumer product.
4. **Then, and only then, pour** — pick the one or two loops your ARPU allows and feed them; paid only when LTV:CAC ≥3 and payback <12 months on an *organic* cohort at real prices.
5. **Then optimise** — NRR, pricing steps, expansion, efficiency ratios.

Steps 1–3 are P0. Step 4 is P1→P2. Step 5 is P2→P3. Doing them out of order is the most common way to lose a year (§6).

### 1.3 Which lever dominates at each stage

| Stage | Dominant lever | Why | Everything else |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P0** | **Product correctness on the ICP's names + activation + early return**, with the founder personally talking to users | The only signal that resolves at n<100; every acquisition hour lands on the first session; it is the mechanism by which a trial converts | is either building the (light) measurement ruler for P1, or is noise |
| **P1** | **One organic loop** + the first churn cohorts | Find the one channel that repeatably produces *activated* users; learn what churn is for this product from the first 30 payers | paid, affiliates, sponsorships are *gated tests*, not programmes |
| **P2** | **Unit economics + the paid gate** | Payback and LTV:CAC become real; annual mix and involuntary-churn recovery are the cheapest wins | NRR still structural (<100% for prosumer) |
| **P3** | **NRR / efficiency** | Growth per dollar; expansion; pricing steps | — |

### 1.4 The model, as ten rules

1. Retention sets the ceiling; acquisition sets the slope; activation is the multiplier between them.
2. Loops beat channels. Ask of every channel: what does one new user *produce* that recruits the next?
3. ARPU chooses the channel, you don't. At $10–20/mo: organic, artifacts, communities, AI-assistant recommendation, integrations. Not paid, not humans.
4. Below 100 users nothing aggregate is readable. Watch, ask, count — don't test. State every P0 target as a count over a fixed denominator, never a rate.
5. One metric per stage; one channel at a time; ≥90 days before judging a compounding channel; a written kill number before starting anything.
6. Founder-hours are the scarce resource, not cash. Cost every lever in hours; cap engineering rather than floor marketing (floors get missed, caps get hit) — and put the cap behind a gate, because a bare cap has already been broken.
7. Everything you publish is proof or it is noise. Third-party > specific first-party > asserted.
8. Descriptive-only copy is not a tax; for a product referred by AI assistants it is the copy that survives into the answer.
9. Know the cost side: gross margin after data licences, and the visit volume the price point requires. A $12-ARPU business is a volume business — judge every channel by whether it can plausibly reach five-figure monthly visits.
10. The company needs a kill/persevere date the way each lever needs a kill number.

### 1.5 The two pieces of arithmetic most playbooks skip — cost side and volume side

**(a) COGS, gross margin, and the licence to operate.** LTV throughout this document is gross profit (~$9.60/mo per payer at ~$12 blended ARPU, `PAID_ADS_PATHWAY.md`), which silently assumes ~80% gross margin. The items behind that number: data-vendor licences (Massive/Polygon Starter and Finnhub Free — both flagged personal / non-commercial use in `docs/LICENSE_AUDIT.md`), hosting (Fly, Neon), email (Resend), Stripe fees (~2.9% + 30¢ → ~$0.59 on a $9.99 charge, ~6%), and any LLM spend (inbox bot capped at $5/day when live). Two facts follow. First, a commercial data licence is a *step* cost, not a per-user one: at licence cost L per month the break-even is L ÷ 9.6 payers before the first dollar of contribution (a $100/mo licence ≈ 11 payers; $200 ≈ 21; $500 ≈ 52 — illustrative, quote needed). Second, until the licence question is closed the business is operating on terms it may not be entitled to, which is a business blocker, not a marketing one. **Stage: P0. Only the lawyer consult and a vendor quote close it.** Tapeline: **not done** — no COGS line exists in any doc; `LICENSE_AUDIT.md` names the exposure; the lawyer brief in §5.1 carries it.

**(b) The required-volume backsolve.** §2.5's arithmetic says a $10k/mo business at $12 ARPU needs ~830 payers. Finish it: to *hold* 830 payers at 5% monthly churn you need ~42 new payers a month; at a no-card trial→paid of 4–6% (ChartMogul × Poyar Jan 2026 "good" band) that is ~700–1,050 new trials a month; at 3–5% visitor→signup that is ~15–35k visits a month, sustained. Cumulatively, reaching 830 payers means on the order of 14–20k trials and 300–600k visits. Consequences: (i) channels are judged by whether they can plausibly reach five-figure monthly visits — SEO, AEO, embeds, an extension listing and a marketplace can; daily Reddit answers and daily X posts cannot on their own (they are inputs to AI citation and SEO, not volume channels); (ii) the annual mix (≥40%) and a higher-ARPU anchor actually sold reduce the payer count needed; (iii) founder-hours per activated user is the ratio that matters until visits are in the thousands. **Stage: P0 (a one-off calculation that reorders the channel table).** Tapeline: **done here for the first time**; not in any prior doc.

## 2. The stage gate — what matters, what proves you've earned the next stage, what is noise

Most published SaaS advice is written for funded companies with a finance function and a sales team. Below ~100 users most of it is *noise*: ratios are 0/0, samples cannot distinguish signal from chance, dashboards measure things that do not yet exist. This section is the filter. It is the most valuable part of the document.

### 2.1 P0 — pre-PMF (<100 users, <$1k MRR)  ← Tapeline

**The six things that matter**
1. **The product is correct on the names the ICP uses it for.** A trust product's published output must be reproducible, factor-derived on the production path, and computed on the securities the buyer actually trades. #507's finding — 6,345 of 8,845 tickers have no price/volume read (~28% coverage), including every current top-scored name, so the public Top-10 is micro-cap-heavy — is a product/ICP-fit defect that outranks every onboarding item. Question to answer with one query: which of the liquid US names a part-time swing trader watches have no price/volume read, and why.
2. **The first session lands.** A signup reaches first value inside a minute, with something real on screen (seeded, defaulted, templated), and the paid feature is *felt* in session one. Reference points: SaaS median activation ~30% (Lenny/Timen 2022, 500+ self-reported responses, B−); PLG average 34.6%, median time-to-value 1 day 12 h (Userpilot 2024, 547 companies, V). At n<100 read this as a count: of the next 50 signups, how many reach the milestone inside 24 h.
3. **The founder personally talks to every user.** Same-day personal email to every signup; 8–12 switch/JTBD interviews under Mom Test rules; a running interview log that records the alternative tool, the workflow moment, the price paid and the words used — never capital, holdings, experience level, or goals against a name (compliance rule 8). This is *the* sub-100-user research method (Moesta: 10–12 interviews surface 3–5 buying patterns, C; Fitzpatrick, C; Superhuman: human-onboarded activation ≈2× self-serve, 2× referral, D with author-flagged selection bias).
4. **A light measurement ruler exists before the first payer:** a `core_action_at` stamp; activation-within-24h counted per signup week; a churn-event ledger with reason codes; a reactivation stamp; the Sean Ellis question emailed at day 7–10 to activated users; one OMTM read weekly by hand from the database with kill criteria written in advance. **Not** a cohort-grid dashboard — at 2–5 signups a week a W1/W2/W4/W8/W12 grid is a matrix of 0s, 1s and 2s for six months.
5. **Positioning is derived, not asserted** — a best-customer table (Dunford step 1), the alternative each named, their words for the value; a hero that says *who it is for*; a for/not-for block.
6. **Founder-made content on the one platform the ICP asks questions in** (for a $20 prosumer tool: YouTube first, then Reddit answers, comparison pages, retail-appropriate directories → AI-assistant citation), plus the artifacts that travel (badge / embed / share card / extension listing), placed on domains the founder does not control. One channel, held 90 days, before the next.

**Metrics that prove you've earned P1 — counts over fixed denominators, not rates**
- Of the next 50 signups, ≥20 activate within 24 h (milestone 1) **and** ≥10 of those 20 either arm an alert or return in week 2.
- Of the first 20 activated users after the change, ≥6 are still active (core action) in week 8 — the "curve flattens above zero" test in count form.
- **≥10 organic paying customers, each retained ≥60 days** (identical to condition 1 of `PAID_MARKETING_PLAYBOOK.md` §4).
- ≥1 unpaid third-party mention on a domain the founder does not control; ≥3 named *workflow* quotes with role.
- Moved to P1 (they cannot resolve below ~100 users): Sean Ellis "very disappointed" ≥40% at n≥40; ≥5 real reviews on the ICP's review platform; ">50% of signups organic" (trivially true at $0 ads — carries no information here).

**Noise at P0 — do not spend hours on it**
MRR growth %, churn %, NRR/GRR, LTV:CAC as a *measured* ratio, CAC payback, magic number, burn multiple, Rule of 40, quick ratio, NPS, A/B tests of anything (~8k visitors/arm to see a 20% lift on a 5% base = years at 50 visits/week), price tests (~1,000 trials/arm), Van Westendorp / Gabor-Granger / conjoint (need 100+ respondents), cohort-grid dashboards, health scores, CS platforms, onboarding tools and product tours, feature-flag services, OKRs, community, affiliate recruitment, sponsorships, PR, events, cold outreach, paid search / social, category creation, "Most popular" badges, user counts, testimonial walls under three named quotes, review badges under five reviews, RICE spreadsheets, roadmaps with dates, a second integration surface before the first one has produced installs.

### 2.2 LATER — not now · P1 — early traction (100–1,000 users, first 10–100 payers, $1k–25k MRR)

**What matters:** (1) one organic loop producing *activated* users repeatably (kill/keep at 90 days each); (2) trial→paid becomes readable at ≥50 completed trials — free trial 8–12% good / 15–25% great (Lenny/Poyar 2023, 1,000+ self-reported, B−; the article does not split card vs no-card); ChartMogul × Poyar Jan 2026 (n=200, B) "good" bands: no-card 4–6%, card 25–35%, freemium 3–5%, reverse trial 4–6% ("great": 10–15 / 50–60 / 8–12 / 8–12; overall median 8%); (3) first churn cohorts: separate voluntary / involuntary / trial-lapse ledgers; churn interviews with the first 5–8 cancels; (4) annual mix ≥30–40% of new paid; dunning verified with real money; (5) reviews ≥5 → then the badge; ≥3 named *workflow* testimonials beside the price; directory pages with reviews; Sean Ellis ≥40% at n≥40; (6) affiliates recruited by hand (10–20 partners) once trial→paid is a number; one $200–500 sponsorship test only after the landing page converts organic traffic; (7) light OKRs only if a second person exists.

**Earns P2 when:** ≥100 payers; monthly logo churn ≤5% blended (≤3% annual-heavy) measured over ≥3 months; a cohort curve flat by M3 at ≥30–40%; LTV:CAC ≥3 and payback <12 months *on the organic cohort*; a message that has out-converted the generic hero organically (ads gate condition 4).

**Noise at P1:** NRR as a *target* (>100% is essentially unobserved under $10–25 ARPA — the ChartMogul figures usually quoted for this are [unverified] here), enterprise sales motions, conferences, PR as a function, health-score models, hiring before support costs >5 h/week, more than one channel added per quarter.

### 2.3 LATER — not now · P2 — scaling ($25k–250k MRR)

**What matters:** payback <12 months (<6 for a bootstrapper with no float); the paid gate re-read with real numbers and turned on only where CAC ≤ LTV/3 (for Tapeline's modelled LTV $106–192 that is ~$35–65 per *paying* user); monthly→annual campaigns at day 60–90; involuntary recovery ≥50% of failed invoices; expansion via tier upgrade and add-ons; a price step ≤20–25% on new customers with 12-month grandfathering; first contractor (support first, when it costs >5 h/week); A/B tests become possible on a page at ≥10k visitors/month; magic number / burn multiple only if capital is taken.

**Earns P3 when:** growth is repeatable across ≥2 channels; NRR measured over 12-month cohorts; a team exists.

**Noise at P2:** category creation (6–10 years, $50–100M — Play Bigger 2016 *(older)* / Dunford; the "~90% of tech IPOs positioned in existing markets" figure is [unverified]), open-metrics dashboards for a non-founder ICP, board rituals without a board.

### 2.4 LATER — not now · P3 — mature (>$3M ARR)

Board-grade metrics apply: NRR (bootstrapped $3–20M median 103%, 90th pct 117.9%; GRR 91% — SaaS Capital 2026, B, n>1,000), Rule of 40, burn multiple <1.5, magic number >0.75, quick ratio >4; bootstrapped median growth 15% YoY, 90th pct 42.3%. This playbook stops here; nothing at P3 is Tapeline-relevant for years.

### 2.5 The bootstrapper fork — why the VC playbook is the wrong one here

Tapeline is bootstrapped, solo, no outside capital — Walling's fork. That changes: (1) every VC-frame metric is off the dashboard; (2) founder-hours are the binding constraint, so every lever is costed in hours (the gap analysis already ranks by EV per founder-hour); (3) payback must be short because nothing floats CAC — hence the ads gate; (4) the product breaks two of Jason Cohen's bootstrapping design rules *(MicroConf 2013, older)* — it is prosumer and low-priced (blended ARPU ≈$12) — so a $10k/mo machine needs ~830 payers at 5–7% churn instead of Cohen's 150 at ~$66/mo. Not fatal (RevenueCat's entire dataset is this class) but it dictates: annual mix ≥40%, zero-CAC distribution only, the volume backsolve of §1.5(b) applied to every channel, and a higher-ARPU anchor actually sold at some point (Trader $59/mo · $588/yr and Team $149 exist as SKUs; the Trader card is the pricing-page anchor on `origin/main`, `frontend/lib/pricing.ts:28-33`).

**The venture-level kill/persevere criterion (new — every lever has a kill number; the company should too).** Write the date now: if by **2027-05-31** (nine months from this document) the P0 exit has not tripped — fewer than 10 organic payers retained 60 days *despite* ≥6 months of a held content cadence and ≥8 interviews recorded — the decision is re-taken in writing among three options: change the product/ICP fit (feed coverage, the liquid-names problem), change the price/anchor (sell Trader/Team by hand), or stop and keep the record running as a public artifact. "Keep going as is" is not one of the options at that date. If the cadence was *not* held, the criterion resets once, and the founder follow-through system (G14) is the problem to fix, not the product.

### 4.2 The one lever that matters most right now — and why

**The founder, in person — and one query.** The in-code half of the first-session fix is shipped (#507). What is left is: the founder emails every signup the same day, offers a 15-minute call, asks one behaviour question, and runs 8–12 switch interviews from that same pool (≈12–15 FH over three weeks, plus 3–4 FH of recruiting); and the founder reads one query — which of the liquid US names the ICP trades have no price/volume read, and why (0.5 FH).

Why this and not something else:
1. **The confirmed defects in code are fixed; the confirmed defects in *knowledge* are not.** Zero interview notes exist; every positioning, pricing and ICP document in the repo is built on inference. Interviews are the only valid positioning, pricing, and PMF method at n=20; the founder-onboarding notes *are* the interview log, the best-customer table, and the VOC sheet (recording alternative tool, workflow moment, price paid and words used — never capital, holdings, experience or goals against a name, rule 8).
2. **The feed-coverage question sits above every remaining onboarding item.** A scanner whose published Top-10 is micro-cap-heavy because the feed does not cover the liquid names is a product/ICP-fit defect that no onboarding change fixes; one query says whether it is a licence problem (A12), a mapping bug, or a universe choice.
3. **It multiplies every other lever.** Every AEO citation, Reddit answer, video, directory listing and referral lands on the first session and on the Top-10. Distribution work done before both are right is wasted at the rate of the activation gap plus the credibility gap.
4. **It is the conversion mechanism of the trial Tapeline already runs.** A reverse trial converts on loss aversion at day 14, which only bites if the user *built something* in 14 days. Pricing is not the lever; the first session and the founder's reply are.
5. **It is cheap, reversible, and un-delegable.** The accounts, the calls, the query and the decisions are founder-only; no agent can do them, which is precisely why they have not been done while ~500 PRs shipped.

After the interviews (≥6 recorded), the one lever *becomes* founder-made cited content — one channel, YouTube, held 90 days — because at that point the constraint is that nothing off-domain mentions Tapeline. Engineering drops to ≤1 day/week and stays behind the interview gate.

### 5.4 LATER — not now · Months 4–12 — gate-driven, not calendar-driven
