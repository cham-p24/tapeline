Tapeline is installed

Here is exactly what it does and what it can see, before you use it.

You'll need a free Tapeline account. After you turn the extension on, click its toolbar icon and follow "Get my connect code" — it takes a few seconds and needs no card.

What it does

When you are on a stock page, a small pill appears in the bottom-right corner showing Tapeline's six-factor score for that ticker. Click it for the factor breakdown, the one-line reason, and — the part no other tool shows — whether Tapeline has published that ticker before and how those picks actually resolved against SPY, losing picks included.

What it reads

  • The ticker symbol in the page address. That is all. On finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA it reads NVDA.
  • It does not read the page itself — no prices, no holdings, no balances, no account numbers, no form fields.
  • It does not track your browsing, and there is no analytics or advertising code in it.
  • It has no account. Nothing identifies you.

Where it runs

It is switched on for public research sites — Yahoo Finance, TradingView, Google Finance, MarketWatch, Seeking Alpha, Finviz, CNBC and others.

Anywhere else, including your broker, it stays off until you turn it on: open the popup on that site and press Enable on this site. That grants access to that one site, and you can revoke it any time in Chrome's extension settings.

Turning it off

The × on the pill hides it for that page. To switch it off on a whole site permanently, open the popup there and press Hide on this site.

What it sends

One request to api.tapeline.io containing the ticker symbol, to fetch that stock's public score and record. The same data is on tapeline.io without any extension at all.

Turned on. You can close this tab.

Tapeline's scores are descriptive readings of published data — not investment advice, price targets or forecasts. Every daily top-10 pick is written to a public record the day it prints and is never edited, including the ones that lose. Read the privacy policy or the full record.
